PTE Essay Writing Guide

← Back to Toolkit

One reusable structure for the PTE Writing Essay task. Learn one flow, adjust a single line depending on the question, and write a complete essay every time.

Step 1 — Identify the Essay Type

Every PTE essay question falls into one of four types. The instruction words in the question (not the topic) tell you which type it is. Once identified, you only need to change one line in the introduction — the rest of the template stays exactly the same. A few swap line options are given for each type — pick whichever feels most natural to you.

Type Trigger words in the question Swap lines to choose from
Opinion agree / disagree, curse or blessing, to what extent 1. "I firmly believe that..."
2. "In my view,..."
3. "I am of the opinion that..."
Advantages & Disadvantages advantages and disadvantages, problems and benefits 1. "This essay will discuss the advantages and disadvantages of..."
2. "This essay will examine both the benefits and drawbacks of..."
3. "This essay will explore the positive and negative aspects of..."
Problem & Solution how widespread, how can we solve 1. "This essay will examine how widespread this issue is and suggest possible solutions regarding..."
2. "This essay will explore the extent of this issue and propose potential solutions to..."
Responsibility who is responsible, whose responsibility 1. "This essay will argue that [government / companies / individuals] bear the primary responsibility for..."
2. "This essay will contend that the main responsibility for this issue lies with [government / companies / individuals]..."
Common trap: some questions look like Opinion questions but are not. "How can we solve...?" and "Who is responsible...?" are not asking for your agreement — they are Problem & Solution and Responsibility types respectively. Always check the exact instruction words, not just the topic.

Step 2 — Turn the Question into a Topic Phrase

The introduction needs a short noun phrase to open with — not the full question. Use this three-step process to extract it.

  1. Strip the question down to its core subject. Ignore instruction words (discuss, explain, agree, how widespread) — those only tell you the essay type, not the topic.
  2. Turn it into a noun phrase. It needs to slot directly into: "___ has become a subject of growing debate in recent times."
  3. Keep it broad, not narrow. A topic phrase that is too specific limits what you can write about in two full body paragraphs.

Worked example

Question: "Many people find that work leaves little time for personal life. How widespread do you think it is? How can we solve the problems caused by the shortage of time?"

Step 1 — core subject: work and personal life

Step 2 — noun phrase: work-life balance

Step 3 — broad enough to cover the whole essay: yes

Two more for practice

Q: "Medical technology is responsible for increasing average life expectancy. Do you think it is a curse or a blessing?" → Topic phrase: medical technology and life expectancy

Q: "As cities expand, governments should look forward to creating better networks of public transport rather than building more roads..." → Topic phrase: public transport versus road expansion in growing cities

Step 3 — The Master Template

This structure never changes. Only the swap line in the introduction changes depending on essay type (Step 1). Notes in italics explain what each sentence is doing — they are for your understanding only and are not part of what you write.

Introduction

[TOPIC] has become a subject of growing debate in recent times.

(Simple factual opening sentence)

While it presents both opportunities and challenges, [SWAP LINE FROM STEP 1 + TOPIC].

(Complex sentence using a 'While...' structure)

This essay will elaborate on this position with relevant reasoning and examples.

(One-line road map for the reader)

Body Paragraph 1

To begin with, [MAIN POINT 1 — first reason / advantage / how widespread / responsible party].

(Topic sentence — be direct)

This is largely because [EXPLANATION — why is this true?].

(Reason — 'because' adds sentence complexity)

For instance, [EXAMPLE — a real situation, place, or consequence supporting your point].

(Evidence)

It is therefore clear that [RESTATE POINT 1 in different words — do not copy].

(Mini-conclusion using a passive structure)

Body Paragraph 2

Furthermore, [MAIN POINT 2 — second reason / disadvantage / solution / secondary party].

(Topic sentence — different opening word from Body 1)

Given that [EXPLANATION — a different angle from Body 1], this point holds considerable weight.

('Given that' — adds sentence variety)

To illustrate, [EXAMPLE — different from Body 1's example].

(Evidence)

Hence, it can be said that [MINI CONCLUSION — link back to your thesis].

(Wrap up)

Conclusion

In conclusion, [RESTATE YOUR POSITION — rephrase, do not copy from the introduction].

(New wording, same meaning as the intro)

The discussion above highlights that [SUMMARY — mention both points in one sentence].

(Synthesis of Body 1 and Body 2)

Therefore, [FINAL THOUGHT — a recommendation, prediction, or call to action related to the topic].

(Forward-looking closing line)

How to Write the Body Paragraphs

Each body paragraph follows the same internal pattern: Point → Reason → Example → Mini-conclusion. This is intentional — PTE scores essays on Content and General Linguistic Range above all else, and this four-sentence pattern protects both.

Content is protected because every sentence in the paragraph loops back to the same point — there is no room to drift off-topic.

Linguistic Range is protected because the six connecting phrases across both paragraphs are all structurally different: While / This is largely because / For instance / Given that / To illustrate / Therefore. Using all six naturally produces a mix of simple, complex, and passive sentences without you having to think about grammar rules while writing.

Important: Body 2's example must be genuinely different from Body 1's example. Repeating a similar example in a different sentence still counts as a content weakness.

How to Fill the Conclusion

The conclusion has exactly three jobs: restate your position in new words, summarise both body paragraphs in a single sentence, and close with a forward-looking thought. Do not introduce a new idea here — only summarise what has already been said.

Restating without copying — the intro and conclusion should express the same stance using different words. For example:

Intro: "I firmly believe that medical technology is largely a blessing..."

Conclusion: "Medical technology is undeniably one of humanity's greatest achievements."

Same stance, completely different wording — this is exactly what raises the Linguistic Range score without any extra effort.

Full Worked Demo

Question: "Many people find that work leaves little time for people's personal life. How widespread do you think it is? How can we solve the problems caused by the shortage of time?"

Essay type identified: Problem & Solution (trigger words: "how widespread", "how can we solve")

Topic phrase: work-life balance

In the demo below, plain text is the fixed template and italicised text is what the student fills in.

Work-life balance has become a subject of growing debate in recent times. While it presents both opportunities and challenges, this essay will examine how widespread this issue is and suggest possible solutions regarding the shortage of time caused by demanding work schedules. This essay will elaborate on this position with relevant reasoning and examples.


To begin with, the problem of work consuming personal time is considerably widespread across modern societies. This is largely because competitive job markets and the rise of remote work have blurred the boundaries between professional and personal life. For instance, in countries like Japan and the United States, overworking has become a cultural norm, with many employees regularly working beyond contracted hours. It is therefore clear that this issue extends far beyond individual experience and reflects a broader global pattern.


Furthermore, one effective solution to this problem is enforcing stricter workplace policies around working hours. Given that many employees feel pressured to overwork due to fear of job loss or professional setbacks, this point holds considerable weight. To illustrate, several European nations such as Germany and France have implemented legal limits on working hours, resulting in healthier and more productive workforces. Hence, it can be said that policy-level intervention can significantly restore balance between work and personal life.


In conclusion, overworking is a widespread and deeply rooted issue in today's professional world. The discussion above highlights that it stems from systemic workplace pressures, yet can be effectively addressed through strong labour policies. Therefore, governments and organisations must collaborate to create environments where productivity and personal wellbeing are equally valued.

Word count: 249 words — within the required 200–300 word range.

Worked Example 1

Question: "Do you believe that video games have a positive impact on cognitive skills?"

Essay type identified: Opinion (trigger words: "Do you believe"; swap line: "I firmly believe that...")

Topic phrase: the impact of video games on cognitive skills

The impact of video games on cognitive skills has become a subject of growing debate in recent times. While it presents both opportunities and challenges, I firmly believe that video games have a largely positive effect on cognitive skills when played in moderation. This essay will elaborate on this position with relevant reasoning and examples.


To begin with, video games sharpen problem-solving abilities and sustained attention. This is largely because many games place players in complex situations where they must think strategically, make quick decisions, and learn from their mistakes. For instance, strategy games such as Age of Empires require players to manage resources and plan several steps ahead. It is therefore clear that the mental skills needed in study and work can be strengthened through regular and balanced gaming.


Furthermore, multiplayer games improve teamwork, communication, and memory. Given that players must coordinate with teammates in real time and remember maps, roles, and tactics, this point holds considerable weight. To illustrate, some studies have found that surgeons who played video games made fewer errors during simulated operations. Hence, it can be said that gaming offers cognitive benefits that reach well beyond entertainment.


In conclusion, gaming, when enjoyed sensibly, is a worthwhile way to develop the mind. The discussion above highlights that games strengthen individual thinking skills and also encourage cooperation and memory. Therefore, parents and educators should encourage balanced gaming habits rather than discourage the activity altogether.

Worked Example 2

Question: "What are the main reasons people struggle to maintain healthy eating habits in today's society?"

Essay type identified: Opinion (this question asks for your view on the main reasons, so use an Opinion swap line: "In my view,...")

Topic phrase: maintaining healthy eating habits

Maintaining healthy eating habits has become a subject of growing debate in recent times. While it presents both opportunities and challenges, in my view, the main reasons people struggle to eat healthily today are a lack of time and the easy availability of unhealthy food. This essay will elaborate on this position with relevant reasoning and examples.


To begin with, busy modern lifestyles leave people with very little time to plan and prepare nutritious meals. This is largely because long working hours and heavy commutes make quick, ready-made food seem like the only practical choice. For instance, many office workers in big cities skip breakfast and order fast food or instant noodles for dinner. It is therefore clear that healthy habits are often sacrificed by people who are trying to save time.


Furthermore, cheap processed food is advertised heavily and sold almost everywhere. Given that fast-food outlets and convenience stores appear on nearly every street and promote tempting deals, this point holds considerable weight. To illustrate, supermarkets often place sugary snacks near the checkout counters, where shoppers are easily persuaded to buy them. Hence, it can be said that an environment full of unhealthy choices makes good eating habits harder to sustain.


In conclusion, eating well has become difficult for many people because of modern pressures and temptations. The discussion above highlights that limited time and the constant presence of unhealthy food are the central obstacles. Therefore, individuals and governments should work together to make nutritious food more convenient and affordable.

Worked Example 3

Question: "What are the advantages and disadvantages of multitasking in daily life?"

Essay type identified: Advantages & Disadvantages (trigger words: "advantages and disadvantages"; swap line: "This essay will discuss the advantages and disadvantages of...")

Topic phrase: multitasking in daily life

Multitasking in daily life has become a subject of growing debate in recent times. While it presents both opportunities and challenges, this essay will discuss the advantages and disadvantages of multitasking in daily life. This essay will elaborate on this position with relevant reasoning and examples.


To begin with, the main advantage of multitasking is that it helps people complete more work in less time. This is largely because handling several small tasks together removes waiting time and keeps a busy schedule moving. For instance, a parent can cook dinner while helping children with their homework. It is therefore clear that valuable time can be saved through sensible multitasking at home and at work.


Furthermore, a major disadvantage is that multitasking reduces concentration and increases mistakes. Given that the brain cannot give full attention to two demanding activities at once, this point holds considerable weight. To illustrate, drivers who text while driving are far more likely to cause accidents. Hence, it can be said that the quality of work and personal safety may suffer when attention is divided.


In conclusion, doing several things at once can be both useful and risky. The discussion above highlights that multitasking saves time on simple jobs but weakens focus and accuracy on important ones. Therefore, people should multitask only with easy tasks and give their full attention to demanding ones.

Worked Example 4

Question: "To what extent do you think that a person's success is due to their natural talent versus their hard work?"

Essay type identified: Opinion (trigger words: "To what extent do you think"; swap line: "I am of the opinion that...")

Topic phrase: the relationship between talent and hard work in achieving success

The relationship between talent and hard work in achieving success has become a subject of growing debate in recent times. While it presents both opportunities and challenges, I am of the opinion that hard work contributes far more to a person's success than natural talent. This essay will elaborate on this position with relevant reasoning and examples.


To begin with, consistent effort is the strongest driver of long-term achievement. This is largely because even highly gifted people cannot improve their skills without regular practice and discipline. For instance, Michael Jordan famously missed out on his school's varsity basketball team, yet he trained relentlessly and became one of the greatest players in history. It is therefore clear that success is built through determination rather than ability alone.


Furthermore, natural talent gives only a head start and does not guarantee results. Given that talented people who stop learning are soon overtaken by those who keep improving, this point holds considerable weight. To illustrate, gifted students who rely on ability alone often fall behind classmates who study steadily every day. Hence, it can be said that talent is useful but remains secondary to effort.


In conclusion, dedication, much more than inborn ability, shapes a person's achievements. The discussion above highlights that effort drives progress, while talent alone cannot guarantee it. Therefore, young people should be encouraged to value persistence and practice above natural gifts.

Worked Example 5

Question: "What potential challenges do students face in online learning environments?"

Essay type identified: Opinion (this question asks for your view on the main challenges, so use an Opinion swap line: "In my view,...")

Topic phrase: online learning

Online learning has become a subject of growing debate in recent times. While it presents both opportunities and challenges, in my view, the main challenges students face in online learning are technical difficulties and a lack of motivation. This essay will elaborate on this position with relevant reasoning and examples.


To begin with, technical problems frequently disrupt online classes. This is largely because many students have unreliable internet connections and limited access to suitable devices. For instance, in many rural areas of developing countries, power cuts and slow connections regularly interrupt live lessons. It is therefore clear that equal access to education cannot be guaranteed when technology fails.


Furthermore, students often struggle to stay motivated and focused when studying alone. Given that home environments are full of distractions and lack the structure of a classroom, this point holds considerable weight. To illustrate, a learner attending a lecture from the bedroom may drift to social media or switch off the camera and lose interest. Hence, it can be said that the absence of face-to-face interaction weakens engagement and discipline.


In conclusion, virtual classrooms bring convenience but also real difficulties for learners. The discussion above highlights that unstable technology and low motivation are the most serious obstacles. Therefore, schools should improve technical support and design more interactive online lessons.

Worked Example 6

Question: "At present, some old people prefer to live in nursing homes instead of living with their adult children. What do you think are the reasons?"

Essay type identified: Opinion (trigger words: "What do you think"; swap line: "I firmly believe that...")

Topic phrase: the choice of nursing homes by elderly people

The choice of nursing homes by elderly people has become a subject of growing debate in recent times. While it presents both opportunities and challenges, I firmly believe that elderly people prefer nursing homes mainly because they receive professional care there and enjoy independence and companionship. This essay will elaborate on this position with relevant reasoning and examples.


To begin with, nursing homes provide professional medical care that families often cannot offer. This is largely because adult children are busy with their careers and cannot look after ageing parents around the clock. For instance, an elderly person with arthritis or memory loss can rely on trained nurses and doctors who are available day and night. It is therefore clear that safety and health are given high priority by many older people when choosing where to live.


Furthermore, older adults value independence and the company of people their own age. Given that many feel lonely or like a burden when living in the busy homes of their children, this point holds considerable weight. To illustrate, residents of nursing homes often play cards, practise yoga, and celebrate festivals together. Hence, it can be said that a lively community can bring older people more happiness than family life alone.


In conclusion, many senior citizens regard nursing homes as a comfortable and respectful place to spend their later years. The discussion above highlights that professional care and social independence are the key attractions. Therefore, society should improve the quality of these facilities while families continue to give their elderly members love and attention.
